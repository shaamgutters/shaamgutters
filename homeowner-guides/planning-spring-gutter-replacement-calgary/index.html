Planning Gutter Replacement in Calgary for Spring

By SHAAM GUTTERS LTDOctober 27, 2026

If you’re planning gutter replacement in Calgary next spring, start by recording where your current system struggles. Photos, drainage observations and a clear project scope give your contractor useful information before installation.

The goal goes beyond choosing a colour. Your new eavestroughs need a complete drainage plan, from the roof edge to the downspout discharge.

Record the problem before requesting a quote

Walk around your home from ground level. Look for loose sections, separated connections, visible damage and water stains beneath the roof edge.

During rain or a thaw, observe where water travels from a safe location. Note whether water spills over one section, escapes at a corner or collects near the house.

Take a wide photo showing each affected roofline, followed by a closer photo from the ground. Include nearby doors, decks and walkways so your contractor understands the surrounding area.

Avoid climbing an icy ladder or walking on the roof for photos.

Include downspouts in your replacement plan

New gutters need suitable outlets, downspouts and discharge locations. Reusing the existing layout without reviewing where water ends up leaves an important part of the project unresolved.

Look at each downspout and ask:

  • Where does the water discharge?
  • Does the extension lead toward a low spot beside the house?
  • Does runoff cross a walkway?
  • Does the route affect a neighbouring property?

Federal flood-prevention guidance recommends directing downspouts toward a well-drained area, such as a vegetated part of the yard, and at least two metres from surrounding foundations where possible. The right arrangement also depends on your property’s grading and local requirements. [1]

Ask your contractor to explain the proposed discharge locations before approving the work.

Separate drainage problems from roof ice problems

Icicles and ice buildup deserve attention, but new gutters alone do not address every cause.

Natural Resources Canada identifies air sealing, insulation and roof ventilation as important parts of preventing ice dams. If you repeatedly see heavy ice along the roof edge, ask for an assessment of the underlying cause alongside your gutter estimate. [2]

Photograph recurring ice from the ground. Record where the buildup appears and whether you notice interior water stains.

Compare the scope, not only the total

Two gutter quotes often include different work. Before comparing prices, check whether each estimate identifies:

  • Gutter size, colour and sections included.
  • Downspout quantity and locations.
  • Extensions or connections included.
  • Removal and disposal of existing materials.
  • Fascia assessment and any separate repair allowance.
  • Cleanup and installation review.
  • Warranty terms and exclusions.

If you also plan roofing, siding or fascia work, tell each contractor before scheduling. Agree on the work sequence so everyone understands which components stay and which require removal.

Confirm your spring installation window

Ask for an estimated installation window and how the contractor handles weather delays. A preferred month helps with planning, but access and suitable working conditions still affect the schedule.

Before work starts, confirm parking, gate access, pets and any areas the crew must protect. Keep the written estimate and colour selection together so both sides refer to the same scope.
